1. Buy this equipment first (about $60-90)

  • 2 metal sheet pans (half-size) and foil or parchment paper
  • Instant-read thermometer (about $15). It tells you when meat is cooked and safe.
  • 1 chef's knife (8 in) and a big cutting board (damp towel underneath so it doesn't slide)
  • 1 pot with a lid (rice, soup) and 1 large skillet
  • 8-10 food containers with lids, measuring cups and spoons (a kitchen scale is a bonus)

2. Six safety rules

  1. Chicken and turkey must reach 165°F in the thickest part (dark meat tastes better at 175°F). Ground beef and pork 160°F. Steaks and pork chops 145°F.
  2. Wash hands after touching raw meat. Don't wash raw chicken.
  3. Use a separate cutting board for raw meat, or wash it well.
  4. Put cooked food in the fridge within 2 hours.
  5. Cooked food lasts 3-4 days in the fridge. Freeze what you won't eat by then.
  6. Thaw in the fridge overnight, never on the counter. Reheat leftovers to 165°F.

3. How to shop

  1. Add recipes to My plan, then use Copy shopping list.
  2. Meat is sold by the pound (lb). Family packs are cheapest; freeze what you won't cook this week.
  3. Compare the unit price on the shelf tag (price per lb or oz), not the sticker price.
  4. Store brands are fine for rice, oats, beans, canned tomatoes, frozen vegetables and milk.
  5. Salt, spices and oil: buy once, they last months.
