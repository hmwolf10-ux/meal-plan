Recipe Book

1. Buy this equipment first (about $60-90 total)

  • 2 metal sheet pans (half-size, 18x13 in) and foil or parchment paper
  • Instant-read thermometer (about $15). This is how you know meat is cooked and safe.
  • 1 chef's knife (8 in) and 1 big cutting board (put a damp towel under it so it doesn't slide)
  • 1 pot with a lid (for rice, soup) and 1 large skillet
  • 8-10 food containers with lids, measuring cups and spoons; a kitchen scale is a bonus

2. Your first week

Pick one protein, one starch, one vegetable, one sauce. Cook them once, mix them into bowls all week.

4 lb boneless chicken thighs + rice + roasted veg + teriyaki bowls + oats

Then open My plan: it shows exactly what to buy. In the Book, tick more recipes any time, and change servings with + / −.

3. The 6 safety rules

  1. Chicken and turkey must reach 165°F in the thickest part (dark meat tastes better at 175°F). Ground beef/pork 160°F. Steaks and pork chops 145°F.
  2. Wash hands after touching raw meat. Don't wash raw chicken.
  3. Use a separate cutting board (or wash it well) for raw meat.
  4. Get cooked food into the fridge within 2 hours.
  5. Cooked food lasts 3-4 days in the fridge. Freeze anything you won't eat by then; it keeps months.
  6. Thaw in the fridge overnight, never on the counter. Reheat leftovers to 165°F.

4. How to shop

  1. Add recipes to My plan; copy the shopping list.
  2. Meat is sold by the pound (lb). Family packs are cheapest; freeze what you won't cook.
  3. Compare the unit price on the shelf tag (price per lb or oz), not the sticker price.
  4. Store brands are fine for rice, oats, beans, canned tomatoes, frozen vegetables and milk.
  5. "To taste / pantry" items (salt, spices, oil): buy once, they last months.
