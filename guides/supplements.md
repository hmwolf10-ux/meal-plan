# Supplements: What Works, What Doesn't

Graded by the IOC 2018 consensus statement and ISSN position stands, with Huberman Lab Wiki suggestions cross-checked. Not medical advice: check interactions and conditions with a clinician. Machine-readable version: [`data/reference/supplements.json`](../data/reference/supplements.json).

## Tier A: strong evidence
| Supplement | Dose | For | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Creatine monohydrate** | 3–5 g/day maintenance; optional loading of about 20 g/day (4 doses) for 5–7 days (IOC) | strength, power, repeated high-intensity work; long-term use up to 4 years without negative health effects (IOC) | cheapest (about $8/month); loading is optional, without it saturation takes about 3–4 weeks; expect 1–2 kg water weight |
| **Caffeine** | 3–6 mg/kg about 60 min pre-exercise (anhydrous); under 3 mg/kg (about 200 mg) can also help when taken during exercise with carbs; over 9 mg/kg adds side effects with no extra benefit (IOC) | endurance (5–150 min) and sprint work | coffee counts; avoid within 8–10 h of sleep; tolerance builds |
| **Carbohydrate during exercise** | 30–90 g/h | endurance events over 60 min | see [`sports-nutrition.md`](sports-nutrition.md) |

## Tier B: good evidence for specific uses
| Supplement | Dose | For | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sodium bicarbonate | 0.2–0.4 g/kg 60–150 min pre (IOC); or split doses over 2–4 days | high-intensity efforts of about 1 min, up to 10 min; about 2% gain | GI upset common and can cancel the benefit; trial in training |
| Beta-alanine | about 65 mg/kg/day (about 4.9 g for 75 kg) as 0.8–1.6 g doses every 3–4 h for 10–12 weeks (IOC) | 30 s–10 min efforts; small 0.2–3% gains | harmless tingling; little for steady endurance; smaller effect in well-trained |
| Beetroot juice / nitrate | 5–9 mmol (310–560 mg) nitrate 2–3 h pre (IOC) | endurance efficiency (4–25% in tasks in some trials), 1–3% in short time trials | smaller effect in highly trained athletes |
| Protein powder (whey) | 20–40 g per serving | convenience | a food, not magic; costs more per gram than eggs, milk or chicken |

## Tier C: situational
| Supplement | When |
|---|---|
| **Vitamin D3** | if blood test shows low; 1,000–2,000 IU typical; Huberman Wiki cites 2,000–4,000 |
| **Omega-3 (EPA+DHA)** | if you rarely eat fatty fish; 1–3 g/day EPA+DHA |
| **Magnesium glycinate** | 200–400 mg evening; modest sleep/cramp evidence |
| **Electrolytes** | long sessions over 60–90 min, hot conditions |
| **Iron** | only if ferritin is low (endurance athletes, menstruating women) |
| **Multivitamin** | insurance for restricted diets; not performance-enhancing |

## Tier D: weak or avoid
NMN/NR (thin human data), BCAAs (redundant with protein), ashwagandha (small studies, quality issues), BPC-157 (not approved, banned in tested sport), "testosterone boosters", fat burners, pre-workouts with proprietary blends (dose caffeine yourself).

## Huberman Lab Wiki cross-check
The wiki (MIT, AI-generated) lists creatine 3–5 g, EPA 1–3 g, magnesium 300–400 mg, vitamin D3 2,000–4,000 IU, beta-alanine 2–5 g, caffeine 3–6 mg/kg, sodium bicarbonate 1/2 tsp, and many longevity supplements (NMN, resveratrol, berberine). Only the performance-related ones above have consensus support.

## Buying rules
1. Prefer single-ingredient products.
2. Look for **third-party testing** (NSF Certified for Sport, Informed Sport).
3. Compare cost per effective dose.
4. Introduce one at a time.
5. Food first: eggs, fish, dairy, and colorful vegetables cover most needs.

## Budget stack
Creatine (about $8/month) + coffee (about $3/month) + good food covers the high-evidence tier for under $15/month.
