# The Complete Chicken Guide

Everything about buying, cutting, cooking, seasoning and storing chicken. Macros marked **(USDA)** come from USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy entries (FDC ids in [`data/reference/ingredients.json`](../data/reference/ingredients.json)). Cook times are approximate; **the thermometer decides**.

## 1. Which cut? (buying guide)

### Skin, bone and yield
- **Bone-in skin-on thigh:** about 60–65% edible by raw weight (bone about 25%, skin about 8%), and roughly **40% of purchased weight** ends up as cooked skinless meat after ~20–25% cook loss. That is why 10 lb of thighs does **not** give 10 oz to 14 people.
- Eating the skin adds roughly 60 kcal and 5 g fat per 6 oz portion. The skin is not crisp after refrigeration; many people peel it before portioning.
- **Boneless** costs about twice as much per lb but nearly all of it is edible.

### Labels
- **Air-chilled** = drier skin, less water lost, crispier result. **Enhanced / "contains up to X% solution"** = pre-brined with saltwater: skip added salt or cut it by half.
- **Free-range / organic / pasture** relate to welfare rules, not macro differences.
- Never wash raw chicken: it spreads bacteria; the heat kills them.

## 2. Knife work

- **Spatchcock (butterfly) a whole chicken:** cut out the backbone with shears, flip, press breastbone flat. Cooks evenly in about 45–50 min at 450°F.
- **Break down into 8 pieces:** 2 breasts (halve crosswise for even size), 2 thighs, 2 drums, 2 wings. Freeze back and wings for stock.
- **Pound thin:** put a breast in a bag, pound to an even 3/4 inch so it cooks evenly.
- **Butterfly a breast:** slice horizontally almost through, open like a book.
- **Cutlets:** slice a breast in half horizontally into two thin cutlets.
- **Debone a thigh:** run the knife along the bone, cut it free; save the bone for stock.

## 3. Doneness and safety

- **165°F (74°C)** is the USDA safe minimum for all poultry, checked in the thickest part without touching bone.
- **Dark meat tastes better at 175–185°F** (thigh, drum, leg): connective tissue breaks down. It stays juicy at that temperature.
- **Breast:** pull at 160°F and rest 5 min; carryover reaches 165°F.
- Juices "running clear" is unreliable; use a thermometer.
- Cool cooked chicken within **2 hours** (1 hour if hotter than 90°F outside). Refrigerate 3–4 days, freeze up to 4 months for best quality; reheat to 165°F.

## 4. Every way to cook chicken

Temperatures and times below are typical; larger pieces need longer.

### Dry heat, oven
| Method | Setting | Best cuts | Times |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sheet-pan roast** | 425°F | thigh, drum, leg, bone-in breast | bone-in thigh 40–45 min · drum 35–45 · bone-in breast 40–50 · boneless thigh 20–25 · boneless breast 18–25 |
| **Roast whole** | 425°F, then 375°F; or 450°F for spatchcock | whole bird | 4–5 lb: 60–80 min whole, 45–50 spatchcocked to 165°F breast/175°F thigh |
| **High-heat roast + broil finish** | 425°F then broil last 3–5 min | skin-on thigh | 160°F then broil to 175°F for crisp skin |
| **Low-and-slow roast** | 300–325°F | whole bird, leg quarter | 1.5–2.5 h; very tender, less crisp |
| **Baked with baking powder skin** | 425°F on rack | wings, drum, thigh | 1 tbsp aluminum-free baking powder per 2–3 lb pulls moisture from skin for a crisp result |
| **Oven-fried (breaded)** | 425°F on rack | thigh, drum, tenders | coat in seasoned flour/egg/crumbs or crushed cereal; 35–45 min bone-in, 15–20 tenders |
| **Broil** | high, 6 in from element | boneless thigh, cutlets | 6–8 min per side; watch closely |
| **En papillote (parchment)** | 400°F | boneless breast, thigh | 18–22 min with vegetables and herbs; steams in its own juices |
| **Stuffed breast** | 400°F | boneless breast | 25–30 min; secure with toothpicks; check center reaches 165°F |
| **Sheet-pan dinner** | 425°F | any bone-in | chicken and potatoes/veg together; give potatoes a 10-min head start |
| **Beer-can / vertical roast** | 375°F | whole | 60–90 min; upright on a can or stand |

### Dry heat, stovetop
| Method | Setting | Best cuts | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pan-sear** | medium-high, 4–6 min per side | boneless thigh, pounded breast, cutlets | dry surface, do not move it until it releases |
| **Sear then oven-finish** | sear 4–5 min, then 400–450°F oven 10–20 min | bone-in thigh, bone-in breast | best crisp skin; ATK method for thighs |
| **Skillet cutlets** | medium-high | breast cutlets | 3–4 min per side; dredge for a crust |
| **Stir-fry** | high, 4–6 min | boneless thigh or breast in 1-inch pieces | marinate with cornstarch ("velveting") for silky texture |
| **Shallow fry / pan-fried** | 350°F oil, 1/2 inch | thigh, drum, tenders | 6–8 min per side for cutlets; bone-in 12–15 min per side |
| **Deep fry** | 325–350°F | bone-in pieces | 12–18 min; buttermilk brine then dredge |
| **Smash / flat skillet under weight** | medium | spatchcock, boneless thigh | "chicken under a brick" for maximum crisp skin |
| **Blackened** | very hot cast iron | breast, thigh | Cajun rub, butter brush, 3–4 min per side |

### Grill, smoke, air
| Method | Setting | Best cuts | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct grill** | medium-high | boneless thigh, breast, tenders | 5–7 min per side |
| **Two-zone grill** | sear then indirect | bone-in pieces | sear 3–4 min per side, finish indirect 20–35 min |
| **Skewers / kebabs** | medium-high | 1.5 in cubes of thigh or breast | 10–14 min turning; soak wood skewers 30 min |
| **Smoke** | 225–275°F | whole, wings, thigh | 2–4 h; finish at 300°F+ for crisp skin |
| **Rotisserie** | 350–375°F | whole | 60–90 min; baste with juices |
| **Air fryer** | 380°F (400°F wings) | thigh 22–28 min · drum 20–25 · wings 20–25 · boneless breast 12–16 | small capacity; flip once |

### Moist heat
| Method | Setting | Best cuts | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Poach** | bare simmer or off-heat | breast | cover with salted cold water, bring to bare simmer, cover, heat off 15–18 min |
| **Braise** | 325°F oven or low simmer | bone-in thigh, drum, leg | brown, then partly submerge in liquid 35–45 min; sauce reduced after |
| **Slow cooker** | LOW 6–8 h / HIGH 3–4 h | thigh 4–5 h LOW · breast 2.5–3.5 h LOW | shreds easily; skin will not crisp |
| **Pressure cooker (Instant Pot)** | high | breast 10 min · bone-in thigh 12–15 min · whole 25–30 min | natural release 10 min; shred |
| **Soup / stew** | simmer | bone-in pieces, carcass | 30–45 min; skim; shred meat back in |
| **Steam** | over boiling water | boneless breast, thigh | 12–18 min; Chinese and dim-sum style |
| **Sous vide** | 145°F for breast (1.5–3 h); 165°F thigh (2–4 h) | breast, thigh | finish with sear; only if you own a circulator |
| **Confit** | 200–225°F in fat | leg quarter | 2–3 h in oil/fat; luxury |
| **Curry / simmer sauce** | medium | thigh, breast | simmer in coconut milk, tomato or yogurt sauce 15–25 min |

### Cold and no-cook
- **Rotisserie chicken (store-bought):** cheapest hot protein per portion at many stores, about $5–8 for ~2 lb of meat.
- **Canned chicken:** shelf-stable emergency protein.
- **Leftover shred:** salads, wraps, quesadillas.

## 5. Brining, marinating, tenderizing

- **Dry brine (best for skin):** 1/2 tsp kosher salt per lb, refrigerated uncovered 1–24 h. Crisper skin, seasoned inside.
- **Wet brine:** about 1 tbsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt per cup of water, 30–60 min for breasts; skip for thighs. Longer brining makes chicken spongy and salty.
- **Buttermilk/yogurt marinade:** mild acid tenderizes; 4–12 h. Do not exceed 24 h.
- **Acid-heavy marinades** (citrus, vinegar) toughen if left over 2–4 h; keep them short.
- **Velveting:** 1 tsp cornstarch + 1 tsp soy + 1 tsp oil per 1/2 lb of sliced chicken; 15–30 min. Silky texture for stir-fries.
- Always marinate refrigerated, discard used marinade or boil it before using as sauce.

## 7. Pairing and meal-prep architecture

- **Batch Sunday, vary the sauce.** Roast one protein, change the rub or sauce by day.
- **Cut for the job:** thigh or drumstick for anything you will reheat; breast for cold salads and quick sautés.
- **Reheating:** microwave covered with a splash of water at 70% power, or oven at 325°F covered with foil; reheat to 165°F. Dark meat is more forgiving.
- **Shred and freeze flat** in bags with a little broth.
- **Never** leave cooked chicken out over 2 hours.

## 8. Common mistakes

1. Cooking straight from the fridge: let it sit 20–30 min or use a lower oven and longer time.
2. Crowding the pan: steams instead of browns; use two pans.
3. Skipping the thermometer.
4. Cutting immediately: rest 5 min (whole bird 15–20 min).
5. Slicing breast with the grain.
6. Salting only right before cooking: salt at least 30 min ahead, or the night before for skin.
7. Too much salt with enhanced chicken.
