# How to Shop: Lists, Quantities, Prices and Budget

## 1. Shopping method

1. **Plan the week first** (see [`meal-prep-system.md`](meal-prep-system.md)): pick proteins, carbs, breakfast, snack.
2. **Check what you have** (fridge, freezer, pantry) before writing the list.
3. **Check the weekly ad** for the store's protein sale. Build the batch around it. Chicken thighs, drumsticks, leg quarters, pork shoulder and ground beef cycle on sale every few weeks.
4. **Write the list by store section** (produce, meat, dairy, dry goods, frozen) to avoid backtracking.
5. **Buy family packs and freeze** the extra in meal-size portions the day you buy.
6. **Compare unit prices** (per lb, per oz, per 100 g): the shelf tag shows it. Bigger is not always cheaper.
7. **Store brand** is nearly always the best value for oats, rice, beans, frozen veg, canned tomatoes, milk, peanut butter.
8. **Don't shop hungry**; use a list; use a weekly budget cap.
9. **Frozen vegetables and canned beans/tomatoes** are cheap, nutritious and waste-proof.
10. **Track actual spending** per week for a month; adjust.

Prices are illustrative; recompute with local prices. Published budget-meal-plan roundups put chicken thighs at about $1.50–2.50/lb (bone-in family packs on sale are lower), rice at about $0.08–0.12 per serving, and dry beans and lentils at about $0.10–0.15 per serving; Budget Bytes' one-pot chicken and rice is listed under $1.50 per serving. At $1.25/lb the base plan assumes a sale price; at $2/lb the 12 lb chicken costs $24, which raises the weekly total to about $59.

## 3. Weekly quantities for the base plan (1 person)

Plan: 7 breakfasts (overnight oats), 14 chicken-rice bowls, 7 shakes.

| Item | Math | Buy |
|---|---|---|
| Bone-in thighs | 14 bowls × 5.5 oz cooked = 4.8 lb ÷ 0.40 | **12 lb** |
| White rice | 14 cups cooked ÷ 3 = 4.7 cups dry ≈ 2.1 lb | 1 bag (5 lb lasts ~2 weeks) |
| Rolled oats | 7 × 3/4 cup = 5.25 cups ≈ 1 lb | 1 canister (lasts 4–5 weeks) |
| Milk | 7 cups (oats) + 7 cups (shakes) = 14 cups | 2 half-gallons |
| Greek yogurt | 7 × 1/3 cup = 2.3 cups (about 20 oz) | 32 oz tub |
| Peanut butter | 7 × 1 tbsp + 7 × 2 tbsp = 21 tbsp ≈ 11–12 oz | 1 jar (16 oz) |
| Whey | 7 × 1.5 + 7 × 1 = 17.5 scoops | existing tub |
| Bananas | 7 × 1/2 + 7 = 10.5 | 10–11 (about 3 lb) |
| Frozen vegetables | 14 × 1/2 cup = 7 cups | 2–3 bags (12 oz) |
| Soy sauce | 14 × 2.5 tsp ≈ 3/4 cup | pantry |
| Honey | 14 × 2 tsp ≈ 1/2 cup + 2 tbsp | pantry |
| Seasoning (rub) | 12 lb bone-in ≈ 2.4× the 5 lb rub | pantry |

Estimated cost at the register: about $50 the first week (chicken $15, milk $7, yogurt $4.50, oats/rice/PB/veg/banana ~$15, seasoning). Later weeks are cheaper when oats, rice, PB and seasoning last several weeks.

## 4. Scaling the shopping list

| Change | Multiply |
|---|---|
| 2 people | ×2 (buy bigger packs; cost per person drops) |
| 4 people | ×4 |
| Cooking 3 days instead of 7 | ÷ 2.3 |
| Swap to boneless thighs | ÷ 1.9 (6.4 lb instead of 12) |
| Add a second protein day | reduce chicken by 2 bowls worth (about 1.7 lb bone-in per bowl-pair) |

Formula: **(servings × cooked oz per serving) ÷ 16 ÷ yield factor = lb to buy.**

## 5. Where to buy what

| Store type | Buy |
|---|---|
| Discount grocers (Aldi, Lidl) | dairy, eggs, oats, canned goods, frozen veg, family-pack chicken |
| Warehouse clubs (Costco, Sam's) | meat family packs, rice, oats, olive oil, frozen veg, butter, eggs, nuts |
| Ethnic markets | spices, rice, beans, soy sauce, produce, whole chickens, cheap cuts |
| Butcher / meat counter | ask for markdown-day packs ("manager's special") and freeze same day |
| Farmers market | seasonal produce late in the day |
| Online | bulk rice, protein powder, creatine |

## 6. Savings tactics
- Buy **whole chicken or leg quarters**, break down yourself; save bones for stock.
- Use **rotisserie chicken** as backup: cheaper than raw at many stores.
- Buy in season; freeze extra bananas (great for shakes).
- Use up produce first; prepare a "clear the fridge" bowl at the end of the week.
- Rotate one **vegetarian** or bean day per week (lentil chili is about $1/serving).
- Cook once, eat twice: leftover roast becomes tomorrow's bowl.
- Avoid pre-cut, pre-marinated and pre-cooked (2–3× price).
- Spices from bulk bins; make your own rubs.
- Track price per 100 g protein in a spreadsheet.

## 7. Pantry staples (cheap, always stocked)
Rice, oats, dry beans and lentils, pasta, canned tomatoes, canned tuna, peanut butter, olive/neutral oil, soy sauce, honey, vinegar, garlic and onion, salt, pepper, cumin, chili powder, paprika, oregano, flour, chicken bouillon or stock, hot sauce.

## 8. Reduce waste
- FIFO: first in, first out; put new items behind old.
- Freeze bread, cooked rice portions, ripe bananas, herbs in oil, stock.
- Label with dates.
- Keep a "eat first" bin.
