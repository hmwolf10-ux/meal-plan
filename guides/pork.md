# The Complete Pork Guide

USDA safe minimum for whole cuts of pork is **145°F with a 3-minute rest**; ground pork is **160°F**; fully cooked ham reheated to **140°F**. Pork is cheap, and shoulder is one of the best value-per-portion proteins for batch prep.

## Cuts

| Cut | Character | Best cooking | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shoulder / Boston butt** | fatty, collagen-rich | slow cooker, braise, smoke, pressure cook | Pulled pork. 8–10 h LOW or 60–90 min pressure. Internal 195–205°F to shred. Cheap: about $2–3/lb. |
| **Picnic shoulder** | tougher, cheaper | braise, smoke | Similar to butt, more bone. |
| **Loin (roast)** | lean | roast at 400–425°F, slice | 145°F then rest. Easy to overcook. |
| **Tenderloin** | very lean, tender, small | roast, sear, grill | 15–25 min at 425°F to 145°F. Remove silverskin. |
| **Chops, bone-in / boneless** | lean loin | pan-sear, grill, bake | Brine 30 min. 145°F + 3 min rest. Thick chops: sear then finish 375°F. |
| **Country-style ribs** | shoulder-cut, fatty | braise, slow cooker, roast | Meaty and cheap. |
| **Spare ribs / St. Louis** | fatty, connective | smoke, oven low-and-slow | 275°F 2.5–3.5 h wrapped and unwrapped. |
| **Baby back ribs** | leaner, tender | oven, smoke | 275°F ~2–2.5 h. |
| **Belly** | very fatty | braise, roast, crisp | Skin-on for crackling; bacon is cured belly. |
| **Ham (fresh/cured)** | cured is pre-cooked | bake, glaze | Reheat fully cooked ham to 140°F. |
| **Ground pork** | fatty (about 20% fat) | meatballs, dumplings, crumbles | 160°F. |
| **Sausage (fresh)** | fatty, pre-seasoned | pan, grill, oven | Cook to 160°F. Precooked: reheat only. |
| **Bacon** | cured belly | pan, oven 400°F 15–20 min | Refrigerate 1 week. |

## Methods

| Method | Setting | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Slow cooker pulled pork** | LOW 8–10 h | shoulder, 3–4 lb; cook with spice rub, onion, 1/2 cup liquid |
| **Pressure cook** | 60–90 min | shoulder cut in chunks |
| **Roast** | 425°F, 20–30 min for loin/tenderloin; 325°F, 3+ h for shoulder | loin, tenderloin, shoulder |
| **Pan-sear** | medium-high 4–5 min per side | chops, cutlets, tenderloin medallions |
| **Grill** | medium-high | chops, tenderloin, ribs (indirect) |
| **Braise** | 300°F 2.5–3 h | shoulder, country ribs |
| **Smoke** | 225–250°F | shoulder, ribs |
| **Stir-fry** | high, thin slices | loin, tenderloin |
| **Carnitas** | braise in orange/lime/spices 2.5–3 h, then crisp under broiler | shoulder |

## Brining and seasoning
- **Chops and loin:** brine 30–60 min (1 tbsp kosher salt per cup water) or dry brine 2–24 h.
- **Rubs:** brown sugar + smoked paprika + garlic + mustard powder (BBQ); fennel + sage + garlic (Italian); cumin + oregano + orange juice (carnitas); five-spice + soy + honey (char siu).
- Pairs with apples, mustard, cider vinegar, sage, thyme, fennel, cabbage, sweet potato.
- See [`seasoning-and-sauces.md`](seasoning-and-sauces.md).

## Meal prep
- Pulled pork freezes 3–4 months in its juices; shred and portion.
- Cook a shoulder on Sunday: 4 lb yields about 8–9 portions of 4 oz cooked, cost about $10–12.
- Carnitas keeps 4 days and crisps well in a skillet.
