# Portions, Measurements and Scaling

How much to buy and cook, raw-to-cooked conversions, and how a recipe changes when you cook 1 serving vs 14.

## 1. Measurement conversions

| Unit | Equals |
|---|---|
| 1 tbsp | 3 tsp = 15 mL |
| 1 cup | 16 tbsp = 8 fl oz = 237 mL (US) |
| 1 lb | 16 oz = 454 g |
| 1 oz | 28.35 g |
| 1 kg | 2.2 lb |
| 1 stick butter | 8 tbsp = 113 g |
| 1 pint | 2 cups; 1 quart = 4 cups; 1 gallon = 16 cups |
| 1 half-gallon | 8 cups = 1.9 L |
| °C to °F | multiply by 1.8, add 32 (200°C = 392°F) |

**Weigh, don't scoop.** A packed cup of flour or oats can vary 20%. A $10 kitchen scale is the best meal-prep tool.

## 2. Raw to cooked (yield)

Cooked weight is not raw weight. Track macros using the state you weigh.

| Food | Raw → cooked | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Boneless chicken breast/thigh | raw × ~0.75 | about 25% water loss |
| Bone-in skin-on thigh → skinless meat | raw × ~0.40 | bone ~25%, skin ~8%, cook loss; range 0.38–0.45 |
| Bone-in drumstick → meat and skin | raw × ~0.45 | approximate |
| Whole chicken → edible meat | raw × ~0.35–0.45 | depends on skin |
| Ground beef (drained) | raw × ~0.70–0.75 | more shrinkage with higher fat |
| Ground turkey | raw × ~0.75 | |
| Pork shoulder (pulled) | raw × ~0.55–0.60 | includes fat and bone loss |
| Chuck roast (braised) | raw × ~0.60–0.65 | |
| Fish fillet | raw × ~0.80–0.85 | |
| Shrimp | raw × ~0.75 | |
| White rice | 1 cup dry → about 3 cups cooked (roughly 3× volume, 3× weight) | |
| Rolled oats | 1 cup dry → 2 cups cooked | overnight oats absorb liquid, ~1.25 cups |
| Lentils | 1 cup dry → about 2.25 cups cooked | |
| Dry beans | 1 cup dry → 2.5–3 cups cooked; 1 lb dry ≈ 6 cups cooked ≈ 4 cans | |
| Pasta | 2 oz dry → about 1 cup cooked; 1 lb dry ≈ 8 cups cooked | |
| Potatoes | ~0.85–0.9 after roasting | |
| Spinach | 10 oz raw → about 1 cup cooked | |

### Example calculation
Goal: 14 portions of 5.5 oz cooked skinless thigh meat.
- Cooked meat needed: 14 × 5.5 oz = 77 oz = 4.8 lb.
- Purchase: 4.8 lb ÷ 0.40 = **12 lb** bone-in skin-on thighs.
- With boneless thighs: 4.8 lb ÷ 0.75 = **6.4 lb**. Compare cost per cooked pound: bone-in $1.25 ÷ 0.40 = $3.13; boneless $3.00 ÷ 0.75 = $4.00.

## 4. Scaling recipes up

### What scales linearly
Meat, rice (dry), oats, beans, vegetables, noodles, milk, eggs: multiply exactly.

### What does not scale linearly
| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| **Salt** | scale linearly for meat by weight; for soups and sauces scale about 75% when doubling, then taste |
| **Strong spices** (cayenne, cloves, cinnamon, allspice, red pepper) | scale about 50–75% when going over 2×, then taste |
| **Herbs, garlic, aromatics** | scale linearly, taste at the end |
| **Liquid in braises and stews** | keep depth constant, not volume; use the same 1/2–2/3 of the way up the meat |
| **Rice water** | 1.5:1 water:rice for stovetop white rice in a tight pot; big batches evaporate less, so drop to about 1.4:1 and check |
| **Thickeners (flour, cornstarch)** | scale linearly |
| **Baking powder/soda** | scale 1:1 but don't exceed 1.5× |
| **Cooking oil** | scale by surface area, not volume |
| **Marinade** | enough to coat, not to submerge |

### Cooking time when scaling
- **Roast/bake:** time depends on **thickness**, not quantity. 12 lb of thighs on four sheet pans cooks in the same 40–45 min as 3 lb on one pan, if pans are not crowded and racks are rotated. Overcrowding adds 10–25% time and steams.
- **Boiling water:** more water takes longer to boil; cooking time for pasta and eggs is the same.
- **Stovetop batches:** a bigger quantity in one pan steams instead of browns. Brown in batches.
- **Slow cooker:** fill 1/2 to 2/3 full; overfilling extends time. Cooking time depends on the size of meat pieces.
- **Braise:** a bigger roast adds about 30–45 min per extra 2 lb.
- **Pressure cooker:** time depends on the piece size, but bigger volumes take longer to come to pressure.
- **Baking dishes:** doubling in a bigger dish increases depth, so lower temperature 25°F and add 10–20% time; or use two pans.
- **Whole birds and roasts:** about 20 min per lb at 325°F for turkey and chicken (rough guide only; use thermometer).

## 5. Scaling down (single serving)
- Use a smaller pan so liquid doesn't burn off; cook time is the same, sauce reduces faster.
- Reduce spice by about the same fraction but keep aromatics generous.
- Eggs: an odd count is fine, beat and measure by volume (1 egg ≈ 3 tbsp).

## 7. Portioning tools
Digital scale, 1-cup measure, 1/2 cup, 1 tbsp, 1 tsp, ice-cream scoop (about 1/4 cup), deli containers, freezer bags labeled by date.

## 8. Recipe scaler
Multiply every quantity by **target servings ÷ recipe servings**, then apply the non-linear rules above. Every recipe in `data/cookbook/` has numeric quantities; the Recipe Book in `index.html` scales them by servings and applies the rules above (salt and strong spices scale about 75% past 2x).
