# Sports Nutrition: Training, Marathons and Everyday Habits

Sources: ISSN position statements on protein, nutrient timing and ultra-marathon nutrition; IOC 2018 consensus on supplements; Huberman Lab summaries from the open-source [Huberman-Lab-Wiki](https://github.com/PatrykWajs/Huberman-Lab-Wiki) (AI-generated summaries, graded here). This is general information, not medical advice.

## 1. Daily targets

| Goal | Protein | Carbs | Fat |
|---|---|---|---|
| General exerciser | 1.4–2.0 g/kg | 3–5 g/kg | 20–35% of kcal |
| Strength / hypertrophy | 1.6–2.2 g/kg | 4–7 g/kg | 20–35% |
| Endurance training (1–3 h/day) | 1.2–1.8 g/kg | 6–10 g/kg | 20–30% |
| Marathon carb-load (last 36–48 h) | – | 8–12 g/kg | low |
| Fat loss | 1.8–2.4 g/kg | whatever's left | 20–30% |

1 kg = 2.2 lb. For a 75 kg (165 lb) person: protein 105–150 g, carbs 225–525 g depending on load.
This repo's example: 160 g protein, 245 g carbs, 68 g fat, about 1,904 kcal.

## 2. Meal timing
- **Protein:** spread 0.3–0.4 g/kg (20–40 g) across 3–5 meals.
- **Pre-training:** 1–4 h before, a meal with carbs and protein, low fat/fiber.
- **Post-training:** protein plus carbs within a few hours; urgency only when training twice within 8 hours.
- Total daily intake matters far more than exact timing.

## 3. Marathon and long-run nutrition

| Phase | What |
|---|---|
| **Training (weekly)** | eat to fuel volume, 6–10 g/kg carbs on heavy days |
| **Race week** | familiar foods, taper volume, keep protein |
| **Carb-load (36–48 h)** | 8–12 g/kg/day, low fiber (white rice, pasta, potatoes, bread, oats, bananas) |
| **Race morning (3–4 h before)** | 1–4 g/kg carbs, low fat/protein/fiber: rice bowl, oats, bagel with jam |
| **During, under 60 min** | water only |
| **During, 60–150 min** | 30–60 g carbs/h |
| **During, over 2.5 h** | up to 90 g/h using glucose:fructose 2:1 |
| **Sodium** | 300–700 mg/h; more in heat or heavy sweaters |
| **Fluid** | drink to thirst; do not over-drink |
| **Recovery** | if another hard session is within 8 h: 1.2 g/kg/h carbs for about 4 h, or 0.8 g/kg/h carbs plus 0.2–0.4 g/kg/h protein (ISSN); otherwise normal meals |
| **Fluid and carbs while running** | 30–60 g carbs/h in a 6–8% solution, 6–12 fl oz every 10–15 min (ISSN) |
| **Before bed (hard training days)** | 30–40 g casein or a slow-digesting protein like cottage cheese or Greek yogurt (ISSN) |

**Gut training:** rehearse race-day fueling in long runs for 4–8 weeks; the gut adapts.
**Sample fuel items:** gels (~20–25 g carb), chews, sports drink (6–8% carbohydrate, about 14–19 g per 8 oz), bananas, rice cakes, dates, pretzels, honey packets.
**Pre-race meal:** see the "Pre-Race / Long-Run Carb Bowl" recipe (Recipes tab, category Bowls).

## 4. Hydration
- Baseline: about 0.5 oz per lb body weight per day (Huberman Wiki heuristic); add for sweat.
- During exercise: weigh before and after; replace about 16–24 oz per pound lost.
- 2% body-mass dehydration impairs performance.
- Hyponatremia risk in long events if you over-drink.

## 5. Budget athlete eating
- Rice, oats, potatoes, bananas, pasta, beans: cheap carbs.
- Eggs, chicken thighs, lentils, tuna, Greek yogurt, milk: cheap protein.
- Commercial sports products are expensive; DIY options: honey-and-salt drink, dates, boiled potatoes.

## 6. Everyday habits from the Huberman Lab Wiki (graded)

| Claim | Evidence grade |
|---|---|
| Protein has a higher thermic effect (about 20–30% of its calories) than carbs (5–10%) and fat (0–3%) | Strong |
| Adherence beats diet style when calories and protein match | Strong |
| Delay caffeine 90–120 min after waking | Plausible, low direct evidence |
| Finish eating 2–3 h before bed | Reasonable |
| 6–8 h eating window raises growth hormone | Weak for outcomes |
| Higher fiber intake associated with more deep sleep | Observational |
| Fructose "damages mitochondria" | Overstated; context-dependent |

## 7. Strength training nutrition
- Protein 1.6–2.2 g/kg; carbs to fuel volume; creatine 3–5 g/day (see [`supplements.md`](supplements.md)).
- Eat enough total calories; a surplus of 250–500 kcal for muscle gain, a deficit of 300–500 for fat loss.

## 8. Food for different goals
| Goal | Adjustments |
|---|---|
| Cutting | prioritize lean cuts (breast, 93/7 turkey, fish), volume vegetables |
| Bulking | thighs with skin, rice, oats, milk, peanut butter |
| Endurance | large rice portions, potatoes, fruit; low-fiber near sessions |
| Budget | eggs, lentils, chicken thighs, oats, rice |
| Vegetarian | eggs, dairy, lentils, tofu, tempeh, rice/legume combos |
